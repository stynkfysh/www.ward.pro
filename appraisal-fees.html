Published starting prices, the same in every market I serve. Your exact fee is quoted and confirmed before you commit to anything.

I publish my fees because the alternative — making every prospective client call for a number — wastes your time and mine. These are starting prices for a single-family home or condominium. Which Product Do You Actually Need?

This is the question worth getting right, and the honest answer is not always the cheaper product.

If the IRS will see it and the property is an improved residence, you need the Standard appraisal. A desktop report does not satisfy IRS requirements for improved residential property. Vacant land is the exception and can be appraised from the desk.

If a court or opposing attorney will see it — divorce, probate dispute, bankruptcy — a desktop appraisal is commonly accepted, and I prepare them for these purposes constantly. Where condition is contested, an inspection is worth its cost because it removes the easiest line of attack.

If the property is outside San Diego or Riverside County, a desktop appraisal is the available instrument, since in-person inspection is limited to those two counties.

If you are not ready for an appraisal at all, the $99 GroundWork report gives you the surrounding sales data, twelve months of trend charts and an unadjusted price range — explicitly no opinion of value — delivered the next business day. If you order a Desktop or Standard appraisal afterward, the $99 is credited against it.

Tell me the property and what the report is for, and I will tell you which tier fits. I would rather talk you into the right product than deliver a cheap one that fails when it matters.

Discounts and Billing

A 5% discount applies to cash-equivalent payment — Zelle, check or cash. Where both spouses jointly retain me in a dissolution matter, a two-client fee applies that costs considerably less than two separate reports. Expert-witness testimony is quoted separately from the report and billed by time.

What These Reports Are For

Everything on this schedule is specialty work: date-of-death valuations and step-up basis, estate and trust administration, gift tax reporting, private party and intra-family transactions, divorce and dissolution, bankruptcy, Proposition 19 transfers, and property tax appeals. Those are the purposes the reports are built for and the readers — the IRS, the courts, counsel, CPAs and fiduciaries — they are written to satisfy.
