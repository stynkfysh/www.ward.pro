National City Property Appraisal — San Diego County, CA

Carved from Rancho de la Nación in the 1860s and reached by the Santa Fe railroad in the 1880s, National City is the county's second-oldest incorporated city and arguably its most industrially entangled. The 32nd Street naval complex, the port's auto terminals and the Mile of Cars line its western half, while the residential grid east of them holds some of the best-preserved Victorian architecture in the region.

How Property Trades in National City

National City's detached stock is old by county standards. The Heritage Square and Brick Row areas preserve 1880s and 1890s Victorians, and the streets around them hold 1900s through 1930s cottages and bungalows on a regular grid with alleys. Postwar expansion added 1940s through 1960s single-story houses eastward toward Euclid and Sweetwater Road and up into the El Toyon and Casa de Salud areas. Lot sizes are modest, two-to-four-unit buildings are woven through the residential fabric, and 1960s through 1980s apartment inventory is significant — small multifamily is a larger share of this market than in most San Diego County cities. Lincoln Acres is an unincorporated county enclave inside the city's footprint with its own zoning and service picture. The Westside and Old Town areas sit close to industrial and port uses, and proximity to freight rail, Interstate 5 and the 32nd Street base is a measurable locational factor. Pricing responds strongly to added units and permitted accessory dwellings. National School District and Sweetwater Union High serve the city.

What Anchors the Area

Kimball Park, Pepper Park on the bay, the Granger Music Hall and the National City Aquatic Center give the city its public spaces. Naval Base San Diego's 32nd Street complex, the Pasha auto terminal, the Mile of Cars dealership district and a broad logistics and light-industrial base supply employment. Southwestern College's higher-education center and Paradise Valley Hospital serve the area. The Blue Line trolley makes three stops in the city and Interstates 5 and 805 and State Route 54 all cross it, which is a real part of the value story.

Valuation Issues Specific to National City

Unit count and legality drive a large share of my National City work. I verify permitted unit counts with the city rather than accepting a rent roll, because converted garages and added rear units are common. Historic designation in the Heritage Square and Brick Row areas affects alteration rights and must be disclosed. Industrial, freight-rail and port adjacency get treated as locational influences with supporting sales, not as generic external obsolescence. For Lincoln Acres parcels I confirm the unincorporated status, since zoning and ADU rules differ from the city's. Deferred maintenance on pre-1940 stock is documented rather than averaged.

How I Serve National City

National City sits in San Diego County, one of the two counties where I work on the ground. That means the full range of products is available here, including the in-person Standard appraisal that the IRS requires for improved residential property.

Whether the assignment is a date-of-death valuation, an estate or trust report, a divorce matter, a bankruptcy filing or a Proposition 19 question, the analysis is built on National City's own comparable evidence rather than on a county-wide average.
