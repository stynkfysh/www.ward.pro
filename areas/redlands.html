Redlands Property Appraisal — San Bernardino County, CA

Few Inland Empire cities kept as much of their original architecture as Redlands. Navel orange money built a substantial Victorian and Craftsman town on the terraces above the Santa Ana wash in the 1880s and 1890s, and the groves held back tract development long enough that the historic fabric survived. The city today pairs that older core with hillside custom housing and newer subdivisions along its eastern and northern edges.

How Property Trades in Redlands

Redlands has a genuinely stratified housing stock, which is unusual for the region. South of the downtown grid, along the Smiley Heights and Sunset Drive terraces, sit large late-nineteenth and early-twentieth-century estates, Craftsman and Mediterranean Revival houses on mature oversized lots, and later custom construction on the slopes toward the San Timoteo and Live Oak canyons. The central grid holds a dense band of 1900s through 1940s bungalows, some in designated historic districts with design review. Lugonia and the areas north of the 10 are mostly postwar and 1960s tract housing on flat lots. East toward Crafton and Mentone, and north along the Mariposa and Judson corridors, the stock turns to 1980s through 2000s subdivisions, with scattered newer infill. Grove remnants and surviving orchard parcels still appear and complicate site analysis. Redlands Unified assignment, University of Redlands proximity, and the mature street-tree canopy all register with buyers, and the hill terraces command a clear premium over the flats.

What Anchors the Area

The A.K. Smiley Public Library and Lincoln Memorial Shrine sit together downtown, with the Redlands Bowl hosting one of the country's longest-running free summer music festivals. Kimberly Crest House and Prospect Park preserve the estate era. Esri is headquartered here and is the city's signature employer, alongside the University of Redlands, Redlands Community Hospital and the Loma Linda institutions nearby. The Arrow service added passenger rail back to downtown and the University station. San Timoteo Canyon and Crafton Hills provide the open-space edges.

Valuation Issues Specific to Redlands

Historic Redlands is the hardest part of this market to appraise correctly. Period houses vary wildly in the quality and completeness of restoration, original detail is a real contributor, and designated districts impose exterior design review that limits what an owner may change. Those properties cannot be bracketed with postwar tract sales a mile north. Hillside parcels bring slope, access and fire-hazard zone issues; grove parcels bring agricultural assessment and irrigation questions. Because my work here is desktop and retrospective, I build condition and restoration conclusions from photography, permit history and sale-era listing records, and I state the limits of that evidence rather than implying I walked the house.
